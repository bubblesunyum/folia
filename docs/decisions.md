# Decision Log

Every product, design and architecture decision for Portfolio Town, with why it was made. **Agents: read this before proposing changes.** If a change contradicts an entry, raise it with @bubbles rather than silently diverging. Record new decisions by appending an entry; mark a replaced one `Superseded by D-0xx` instead of deleting it.

Format: **Question → Decision**, then *Why* and any *Deferred / later* notes. Dates are when the decision was made.

## 2026-09-27: Initial grilling session

### D-001 Where does geometry come from?
**Decision:** A parametric kit generated by **Blender Python scripts** that the agent writes, run headless. Hand-modeling in Blender remains an escape hatch for hero pieces.
*Why:* It makes low-poly ↔ high-poly and the LOD tiers a parameter change plus a rebuild, and it keeps the pipeline reproducible. The alternatives were pure in-code three.js geometry (weak bevels/curves, hard to bake AO), a bought kit (someone else's look) and hand-modeling everything (slowest).

### D-002 The Blender MCP versus scripts as source of truth
**Decision:** Use a Blender MCP for **live art direction only**. The source of truth is Python scripts committed in the repo (`assets/blender/**`). A headless `blender -b -P build.py` regenerates the kit, bakes AO, emits LODs and runs the gltf-transform export. Anything liked in a live session must be written back into the scripts.
*Why:* A live MCP session's state isn't reproducible.
*Note:* Blender and the MCP were not installed on the dev machine as of this date.
*Refined by:* D-034 (params/procedures split, pinned version, exporter contract, fast loop).

### D-003 App framework
**Decision:** **Vite + React Router in framework mode with `prerender`**, fully static output. One persistent `<Canvas>` lives in the root layout. (Started as RR v7; see R-008 for v8.)
*Why:* It needs prerendered HTML per route, a canvas that never remounts, client routing kept in sync with the camera, and a small shell for the 1.5 s first frame. Next.js adds RSC and weight for no benefit, and Astro fights a persistent stateful canvas.

### D-004 URL model
**Decision:**
- URLs are **short, human slugs**: `/` is the town overview, `/<project>` a neighborhood, `/<project>/<case>` a case study. There's no `/work/` prefix and no separate "sky" place.
- Slugs are unique within their parent. Top-level slugs can't collide with reserved words (`resume`, `contact`, `simple`, …), and the build fails on a collision.
- A URL addresses a **Place** with a preset camera. Pan, zoom and look-around form an ephemeral **offset**, never written to the URL, and reset on arrival.
- Entering a Place **pushes** history. Escape or rising navigates to the parent URL, which is also a push.
- Gallery stops (later) use `?stop=N` via `replaceState`.
- Hover never touches the URL.

*Deferred / later:* shareable camera angles. Camera state is modeled as a serializable `{ place, offset }` so a future `?cam=` param is only an encode/decode.

### D-005 Single-case projects
**Decision:** A project with exactly one case study redirects from `/<project>` to `/<project>/<case>`, and Escape skips back to `/`. That's the "no lobby levels" rule. **Exception:** a neighborhood rich enough to be worth seeing stays a real Place. (Cortico has 3 case studies now, so the rule doesn't apply to it.)

### D-006 Wheel and trackpad mapping
**Decision:**
- Two-finger swipe → pan.
- Pinch (`ctrl+wheel`) → zoom, continuous within the Place's limits.
- Drag → pan (look-around at gallery stops).
- Escape → rise one level.
- **Zooming out past the Place's minimum rises one level after a resistance detent.**
- Every wheel event is treated as a trackpad gesture.

*Why:* In the original spec "scroll out = rise" conflicts with trackpad panning, and a detent prevents accidental exits.
*Deferred:* detecting discrete mouse-wheel notches. The owner uses a trackpad and a Magic Mouse, which both send smooth deltas.
*Refined by:* D-048. The mapping stands, but Safari delivers pinch as `GestureEvent`, not `ctrl+wheel`.

### D-007 Camera projection
**Decision:** **A perspective camera throughout, with FOV as part of every camera preset.** The town uses a long lens (~15–20°) for a near-isometric read, flights widen the FOV as they descend (a subtle dolly-zoom), and neighborhoods and galleries sit at ~45–55°.
*Why:* Orthographic-to-perspective blends pop. A long lens gives about 95% of the iso look plus horizon parallax over the forest, which an ortho camera can't show.

### D-008 Rotation
**Decision:** **Yaw is fixed at town level.** At a neighborhood it's a limited orbit (~±35° yaw with a little pitch) that springs back to the preset. Gallery stops (later) get free look within a cone. Yaw is a per-preset `yawRange` (`[0,0]` at town).
*Why:* One art-directed composition means only building fronts must be gorgeous, and it gives a tighter shadow fit and predictable culling.
*Later:* free town rotation may be enabled by config.

### D-009 Milestone 1 neighborhood and content mapping
**Decision:** **Cortico** is the Milestone 1 neighborhood. The art direction is a solarpunk complex of interconnected buildings of varied sizes and functions: housing, food growing, curated and wild greenery, and outdoor leisure. At the center is an upgraded solarpunk **forum** (a gathering and talking place, fitting Cortico's conversation platform). Everything is touched with magic, and even simple objects feel amplified.
In the middle of the forum stand **three pedestals**, one per case study, each holding a stylized object:

| Pedestal | Case study slug |
|---|---|
| Stylized laptop | `/cortico/platform` |
| Stylized smartphone | `/cortico/recorder` |
| Audio glyph (medley maker) | `/cortico/medley` |

The rest of the complex is scenery. Keep it simple so recruiters can navigate fast. The content is seeded by splitting the existing Cortico case study, and @bubbles will rewrite it later.

### D-010 How much of pedestal → panel is in Milestone 1
**Decision:** The **real flow without the magic.**
- Clicking a pedestal navigates to `/cortico/<slug>`, and the camera eases toward that pedestal.
- A real DOM panel opens with a simple opacity/blur reveal and real HTML.
- A **placeholder glowing wisp** (point light, sprite and bob) stands in for Poppy.
- Everything sits behind a `PanelPresenter` seam, so Poppy and the particle materialize can slot in later without touching routing or content.

*Later:* Poppy herself and the particle "quantum soup" materialize and dissolve.

### D-011 Surface style
**Decision:** **Smooth stylized**: modest triangle counts, smooth normals, generous 2–3 segment bevels and clean primitive-based forms. "Low-poly" means a triangle *budget*, not a faceted look. Bevel segments and curve resolution are script parameters per LOD tier.
*Later / experiment:* faceted or chunky foliage with smooth architecture.
*Refined by:* D-028 (stylized-real).

### D-012 Shading model
**Decision:** **Stylized PBR.** Metal/roughness materials are lit by the sun plus an env map generated from our own sky gradient. There are about 6–8 shared materials (gold, cream, glass, a few bold paints, foliage, neon emissive), each extended by a shader-injection layer: per-instance color, sway, hover lift/glow and the reveal dissolve. There are almost no image textures.
*Later / experiment:* toon-ramped foliage (hybrid). Keep foliage materials swappable.
*Refined by:* D-031 (AO and night light as vertex attributes), D-040 (env map, no hemisphere light), D-045 (foliage shading).

### D-013 Time of day in Milestone 1
**Decision:** Build the **time-of-day gradient system now**. Sun angle, sky, fog, hemisphere light, env map and neon intensity are all driven by one gradient, with **4 art-directed keyframes** (dawn, midday, golden hour and night), a `?time=HH:MM` override and a dev scrubber. The Milestone 1 default is a fixed hero time, **golden hour**.
*Later:* switching to the visitor's clock and timezone is a one-line change.
*Partly superseded by D-037:* Milestone 1 art-directs 2 keyframes (golden hour and night); dawn and midday interpolate until later.

### D-014 What `/` shows in Milestone 1
**Decision:** The **whole town skeleton**: terrain, a river and connective paths, and all 9 plots. Cortico is the only built one, and the other plots are shown **under construction** (scaffolding, gold cranes, half-grown gardens). **Cortico is front and center**, and it's where visitors land facing. Plot positions live in content data (`townLayout`, later `pathLayout`).

### D-015 Which projects become neighborhoods
**Decision:** 9 neighborhoods:
1. Cortico
2. Purple Republic
3. Iron Ox
4. Express Your Mess
5. Express Your Yes
6. Glyphite
7. Blackjack Genius
8. **Art** (the 5 art pieces as their own district)
9. **Early work**: Amazon, Iodine, Third & Loom and Bubbles Builds merged into one neighborhood of posters and billboards

"ux cert" is dropped because it has no content.
*Why:* Merging avoids near-empty lots, and an Art district expresses "artist • designer • engineer" spatially.

### D-016 Town layout principle
**Decision:** A **chronological river**. A canal winds from the horizon (oldest: Early work) to the foreground (newest: Cortico at the mouth). **Art sits on a hill off the river**, visible from everywhere, as the town's cultural landmark. The mobile path order follows the river.

### D-017 Water rendering
**Decision:** A stylized water shader: sky env reflection, Fresnel, and neon picked up as emissive streaks. **No planar reflection pass**, because it would double draw calls.
*Superseded by D-039.*

### D-018 Loading reveal in Milestone 1
**Decision:** A **simple version**.
- The sky gradient and a cream "ocean" plane with a wave shader render first (under 1.5 s).
- When the town is ready, the land rises through the cream. Everything above the cream line renders normally, and a thin glossy cream band just above it fades upward, which reads as liquid flowing off.
- It's capped at about 2.5 s, shortened on repeat visits and deep links, and cut under reduced motion.
- Every material carries a `revealHeight` uniform from day one.

*Later:* drips, flow-down streaks and splash particles.
*Refined by:* D-044. The band is a color/roughness blend, never a `discard`.

### D-019 Content format
**Decision:** **MDX with Zod-validated frontmatter**, one folder per project (`content/<project>/index.mdx` plus one MDX per case study plus `media/`).
- The frontmatter's `kind` selects a renderer from the registry (`panel`, `gallery`, `poster`, …).
- The MDX body is the HTML content layer: one source for crawlers, screen readers and the panel.
- Images are build-optimized to AVIF/WebP.

### D-020 Verifying the performance budget
**Decision:**
- A `?perf=base` proxy mode renders at about 2× pixels and caps at 60 Hz to approximate a base M-series Air.
- The HUD shows FPS, frame time, draw calls, triangles and texture memory.
- On the dev machine the target is ≤ ~4 ms frame time at native resolution.

*Context:* the dev machine is an M1 Max (32-core GPU, 120 Hz ProMotion) plus an iPad Pro M4 for touch. There's no low-end hardware available.
**Quality tiers go up as well as down.** Strong GPUs get 120 Hz (Chromium/Firefox only, see R-002), a higher DPR, higher-res bloom and bigger shadow maps. Weak GPUs step down: DPR, then shadows and bloom, then a 60 Hz cap.
*Superseded by D-035 (how the budget is measured) and D-036 (tier ladder).*

### D-022 Panel layout
**Decision:** A **side sheet on the right** (~45% of the width, max ~640px of readable measure) that scrolls internally. The camera reframes with `setViewOffset` so the scene sits centered in the uncovered **left** area. The world stays live and hoverable. Below ~900px it becomes a bottom sheet. There's a visible one-word **"Close"** button, and Escape, the zoom-out detent, clicking the wisp or clicking empty world also close it.
*Refined by:* D-050 (view-offset details, no `backdrop-filter`).

### D-023 Wisp / Poppy position
**Decision:** The wisp (later Poppy) **perches on the panel's top-right edge**, like she's holding it open. Her neon breadcrumb sign ("cortico › platform") sits right above the content.
*Superseded:* the original "Poppy flies to the top-left" idea, because the panel moved to the right.

### D-024 Palette
**Decision:** It's built on the existing brand. `palette.ts` is the single source for both the 3D materials and the panel CSS.
- **Structure:** gold and cream.
- **Bold paints:** tangerine `#F19E4B`, butter `#EFEA5D`, mint `#4BFED2`, lavender and hot pink.
- **Neon:** **one signature color per neighborhood**, e.g. Cortico **mint**, Purple Republic violet, Iron Ox butter, Art hot pink. Poppy's sign takes on the current neighborhood's color.
- **Nature:** a forest-green base (`rgb(0,30,23)`), with water carrying the teal-to-lavender gradient at dusk.

### D-025 Typography
**Decision:**
- **Parkinsans** (OFL) for panel body and headings. Headings are lowercase, with no eyebrows.
- **In-world neon signs** use Parkinsans lowercase as SDF text (troika / drei `<Text>`) with an emissive tube shader.
- **Optician Sans** is for **buttons** only, e.g. "Close".
- The pixel accent (HUD, construction signs) is an **OFL pixel font** (Silkscreen or Pixelify Sans, chosen visually). Player Sans Mono 8x8 is dropped because its license is unknown.

### D-026 Hosting and domains
**Decision:** **Vercel.** During development, previews go to a `noindex` subdomain (e.g. `town.bubblesbuilds.com`). At launch the town takes over `design.bubblesbuilds.com`, and the current Framer site moves to **`vintage.bubblesbuilds.com`**, which is both the WebGL fallback target and where the Simple portal points. The old Framer slugs (`/cortico`, `/webtoy`, `/glyphite`) redirect to the new routes.

### D-027 Visual references
**Decision:** @bubbles supplied reference boards (the "folia" Pinterest board). They're distilled into 7 art pillars in [art-direction/README.md](art-direction/README.md). The direction is close to the proposed Googie, Hadid and Gardens-by-the-Bay mix, but **less toony than Monument Valley or Townscaper**.

### D-028 Fidelity target
**Decision:** **"Stylized-real"**: clean forms, real materials, soft light and lush detail, with **detail spent by distance**.
- The town view uses mid-LOD buildings and clustered foliage clumps.
- The neighborhood vantage streams in high-LOD hero geometry: the forum lattice canopy, flowing terraces, dense planting and neon edge-lines.
- Voronoi canopies and curved terraces are generated procedurally by the Blender scripts (bmesh / geometry nodes).
- The `/cortico` high-LOD stream (~2–4 MB) preloads on hover-intent or idle.
*Refined by:* D-047. The `/cortico` stream is on the critical path for `/cortico/*` deep links and must fit that route's budget.

### D-029 Tooling defaults
**Decision:**
- TypeScript strict, pnpm, Vitest and Biome.
- Playwright for route, HTML and deep-link tests.
- A private GitHub repo, created only when @bubbles asks.

## Research-driven technical defaults (2026-09-27)

These come from [research/perf-best-practices.md](research/perf-best-practices.md), which has sources and versions.

- **R-001 Renderer:** `WebGLRenderer` (three r186), R3F 9 and pmndrs postprocessing 6.x. **No TSL or WebGPURenderer**: on its WebGL2 fallback it doesn't run pmndrs postprocessing, `onBeforeCompile` or `ShaderMaterial`. Revisit WebGPU after launch.
- **R-002 120 Hz:** Safari caps page rendering near 60 fps even on ProMotion, and iPadOS Low Power Mode drops to 30. The 120 Hz tier applies only to Chromium and Firefox.
- **R-003 Tier detection:** a frame-time probe can't reveal headroom (rAF is vsync-locked), so **upgrading a tier needs a short stress probe**. `@pmndrs/detect-gpu` can't tell Apple chips apart in Safari, so use it only as a prior.
  - *Refined by D-036:* probe after the reveal while idle, and cache the tier.
- **R-004 Batching:**
  - **One `BatchedMesh` per shared material**, so about 7 main plus 7 shadow draws for the whole town. Per-instance hover, reveal and seed data live in a `DataTexture` indexed by `getIndirectIndex(gl_DrawID)`.
  - `InstancedMesh` for trees.
  - Every kit mesh must share an identical attribute layout, so export with meshopt `quantizationVolume: 'scene'`.
  - Firefox lacks multi-draw, which is an acceptable fallback.
  - *Revised by D-032 (merge unique architecture per neighborhood × material × LOD; one batch per material still stands) and D-033 (the quantization volume does not set attribute layout).*
- **R-005 Shader injection:** `onBeforeCompile` through a small feature composer with `customProgramCacheKey`. Apply the same injection to `customDepthMaterial` so sway and dissolve show up in shadows.
  - *Refined by D-044:* no `discard` in shared materials.
- **R-006 Post and tone mapping:**
  - **Khronos PBR Neutral** tone mapping (ACES and AgX wash out neon and gold).
  - Bloom uses `mipmapBlur`, which already starts at half resolution; `resolutionScale` is ignored, so tune `levels` (5–6).
  - Set `multisampling` explicitly, because `@react-three/postprocessing` defaults to 8.
  - *Refined by D-038 (bloom driven by time of day), D-042 (AA), D-043 (R3F defaults) and D-046 (grade and fog).*
- **R-007 Shadows and env:**
  - One hand-fitted, texel-snapped directional shadow map, updated only on pan or sun change. No cascaded shadow maps (CSM). Map size 1024 / 2048 / 4096 by tier.
  - The env map comes from the sky rendered to a small cube, then `pmrem.fromCubemap(tex, reusedTarget)`, only when the sun moves about 1°.
  - *Superseded by D-031 (baked AO and night light), D-038 (night lighting), D-040 (env at 256 px from an env scene) and D-041 (shadow policy).*
- **R-008 React Router v8** shipped in June 2026, so start on v8. It's the same architecture as D-003.
- **R-009 Assets:**
  - Geometry uses **Meshopt**, not Draco (about 7 KB against 59 KB decoder), through a custom gltf-transform script rather than `optimize`, whose defaults would simplify, join and palette the meshes.
  - **No KTX2 for now**: the transcoder is about 217 KB and the art style barely uses textures.
  - Load with three's own `GLTFLoader`, not drei's `useGLTF`.
  - *Refined by D-033:* pin `level: 'medium'`, normals ≥ 10 bits, no `instance()` for batched geometry.
- **R-010 Forest:** the fixed yaw means distant trees can be **single-view baked cards** lit by the dynamic sun. No octahedral impostors needed.
  - *Refined by D-050:* keep vantages from framing the cards at a new pitch.
- **R-011 Streaming:** each neighborhood gets its own Suspense boundary, and navigation uses `startTransition`. At about 3 MB total, don't bother unloading. `useLoader.clear` doesn't free GPU memory anyway.
  - *Refined by D-047:* holds for Milestone 1; revisit unloading at 3+ neighborhoods.

## 2026-09-27: Spec review adopted

Source: [reviews/2026-09-27-spec-review.md](reviews/2026-09-27-spec-review.md). In it, B-x is a blocker, S-x should-fix, C-x consider and N-x a nitpick. @bubbles accepted the review in full, including the items that overturn D-013, D-017 and D-020. Where the review left a choice open, the entry below picks a default and names the spike that confirms it.

### D-030 Adopt the spec review
**Decision:** Accept every blocker, should-fix, consider and nitpick item in the 2026-09-27 review. D-031 to D-050 turn them into decisions, and the earlier entries they change are marked.
*Why:* The review found gaps in the Blender-to-three contract, the perf proxy and the night, water and foliage techniques. @bubbles agreed with its reasoning.

### D-031 Baked lighting lives in vertex attributes (B-1, S-4)
**Decision:**
- AO is baked in Cycles into a float vertex attribute **`_AO`**, **in placed context**. build.py assembles the neighborhood from its layout, bakes, then splits it for export.
- The material injection routes `_AO` through three's `aomap_fragment` path. It darkens only indirect diffuse and drives `computeSpecularOcclusion`. It never multiplies direct sunlight, so it can't be `COLOR_0` with `vertexColors`.
- A second bake, **`_NIGHT`** (RGB), stores emission-only light spill from neon, lanterns and windows. The shader adds it to indirect diffuse, scaled by the gradient's night weight.
- Terrain gets ground-contact AO ("dirt") in its vertex colors or in one low-res ground texture.
- Bevel and edge-loop density, where AO gradients need it, is a script parameter per LOD.

*Why:* `aoMap` textures would force one AO atlas per material across every neighborhood, and per-piece bakes miss the contact occlusion the refs depend on. This keeps D-012's "no image textures on buildings".

### D-032 Geometry granularity: merge unique architecture, instance true repeats (B-2, C-5)
**Decision:**
- **Unique architecture** is merged at build time into one geometry per *(neighborhood × material × LOD)*. It goes into the per-material `BatchedMesh` as a handful of instances.
- Sub-object identity (each building, each pedestal) is a per-vertex **`_ID`** attribute. Hover, glow and lift state live **per group** in a small uniform array or a tiny texture, so a hover is one uniform write plus a CPU spring.
- **True repeats** (trees, planters, lamps, seating, crane parts) stay instanced: `InstancedMesh`, or `BatchedMesh` for mixed props.
- The HUD shows `renderer.info.render.calls` **and** the sum of multi-draw sub-draws across batches. Both are budgeted.

*Why:* The refs' buildings are one-offs. Kit instancing would save little but costs per-instance culling and sorting in every pass. On ANGLE/Metal (Safari, Mac Chrome) multi-draw is a loop of real draws, and Firefox has no `WEBGL_multi_draw` at all.
*Replaces:* R-004's framing of kit pieces instanced by `townLayout`. One `BatchedMesh` per shared material still stands.

### D-033 Asset attribute contract (S-1)
**Decision:**
- Each batch has one attribute schema. A **build-time validator** fails the export on any primitive whose attribute set, component type or `normalized` flag differs from its batch's schema.
- At load, geometry is **dequantized to Float32** before it goes into a `BatchedMesh`. That costs a few MB of VRAM at this scale. It removes two traps: the first geometry setting the storage type, and lossy conversion of later ones.
- gltf-transform `meshopt` is pinned to `level: 'medium'` with normals at ≥ 10 bits. When a volume spans a placed neighborhood, `quantizePosition` is 16.
- Nothing headed into a `BatchedMesh` goes through gltf-transform `instance()`.
- For true repeats, the per-instance DataTexture is indexed by `getIndirectIndex(gl_DrawID)` in the vertex stage, and the index is passed on as a `flat` varying (N-1).

*Why:* The quantization volume doesn't set the attribute layout. Out-of-range UVs or custom attributes silently stay Float32, which makes `BatchedMesh` throw. The default `high` level also crushes normals to 8 bits, which bands on glossy cream and gold.
*Replaces:* R-004's `quantizationVolume: 'scene'` reasoning.

### D-034 Blender pipeline contract (S-13)
**Decision:**
- **Procedures and parameters are separate.** The Python in `assets/blender/**` holds the procedures, and each asset has its own JSON params file. The MCP or a small add-on panel edits the params, so keeping a live tweak is a file save, not a code edit.
- **The Blender version is pinned** to the current LTS at install time and recorded in `assets/blender/VERSION`. build.py refuses to run on any other version.
- **Exporter settings are fixed in code:**
  - `export_apply=True`.
  - Color attributes are exported explicitly, using the 4.2+ option.
  - Custom attributes use `export_attributes=True` and a leading underscore.
  - Batch, LOD, `_ID` groups and hit volumes go in `export_extras`.
- **Conventions:** objects are named `<hood>.<object>.<material>.<lod>`, units are metres, and +Y is up after export.
- **Techniques for the hard forms:**
  - **Voronoi canopies:** remesh, triangulate, Dual Mesh (Geometry Nodes), inset and delete faces, solidify, subdivide.
  - **Terraces:** curve-swept profiles with per-level offset noise.
  - **Shells and petals:** SDF/metaball or voxel remesh, then decimate.
  - No `scipy` unless it's vendored.
- **Fast loop:** per-asset content hashing of script plus params, a watch mode, and a Vite plugin that hot-swaps the `.glb` in the running scene. The target is **under 10 s from saving a param to seeing pixels**.
- **Look-dev happens in the browser.** Eevee won't match three's shading, tone mapping or bloom. Material and lighting params live in a live tweak panel (leva or tweakpane) that writes back to `palette.ts` and the time-of-day keyframe JSON.

*Refines:* D-001, D-002.

### D-035 How the performance budget is measured (B-3, N-3)
**Decision:**
- The primary metric is **GPU ms** from `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` in Chrome on the M1 Max (stats-gl `trackGPU`), plus CPU ms. rAF frame time is for display only.
- **Provisional budget:** ≤ 3 ms GPU on the M1 Max, rendering at the base Air's pixel count (2560×1600), until spike 3 calibrates it.
- `?perf=base` renders at exactly that pixel count, caps at 60 Hz, and flags GPU ms against the calibrated budget.
- **The proxy is calibrated once against real hardware:** a base M-series Air, one mid-range Android (e.g. a Pixel 6a or Galaxy A5x) and an iPhone 12 or 13. The Max-to-Air factor is recorded here. Where those devices come from is still an open question.
- **Draw budget:** under ~100 `render.calls`, with a sub-draw budget set during spike 3. Glass, neon and water don't cast shadows, so the shadow pass is smaller than the main pass.

*Why:* The Max has about 4× the ALU and 6× the bandwidth of a base Air. This plan is bandwidth-heavy (bloom, HalfFloat targets, shadow fill), and rAF on a vsync-locked display can't show headroom.
*Replaces:* D-020's proxy definition and its "≤ ~4 ms" target.
*Refined by:* D-051. Real-device calibration is deferred until before launch.

### D-036 Quality tier ladder (S-12, C-6, C-7, C-10, C-14, N-7)
**Decision:**
- **Stepping down**, in order:
  1. Cap 120 Hz to 60 Hz on high-refresh displays.
  2. Lower the DPR.
  3. Turn shadows off with intensity 0 and no map updates (see D-043).
  4. Turn bloom off. Neon falls back to its built-in fake glow (D-038).
- **Stepping up** spends headroom on MSAA, a 4096 shadow map, the water reflection pass (D-039) and, later, half-res SSAO (e.g. N8AO). It never raises DPR above 2.
- **120 Hz runs only in Chromium and Firefox on the Max, and only while the camera moves.** It's never tested on the iPad, because Safari holds it near 60 Hz.
- The upgrade stress probe runs **after the reveal, while idle**. The chosen tier is cached in `localStorage`, keyed by renderer string and screen.
- **Power:** when idle (no input for N seconds) or while a panel is being read, ambient motion drops to ~30 fps.

*Replaces:* D-020's ladder order and its "higher DPR" upgrade.

### D-037 Time of day in Milestone 1: two keyframes (S-12)
**Decision:**
- The gradient system is still built now, but Milestone 1 art-directs only two keyframes: **golden hour** (the default) and **night** (the neon test). Dawn and midday interpolate until they're art-directed later.
- The gradient drives sun angle, sky, fog, the env map, the emissive multiplier, bloom intensity and threshold, and the grade parameters (D-038, D-046).
- `?time=` and the dev scrubber stay.

*Why:* "Every hour must look good" roughly doubles the look-dev work before the core look is proven.
*Replaces:* D-013's "4 art-directed keyframes in Milestone 1".

### D-038 Night, neon and bloom (S-3, S-4)
**Decision:**
- The time-of-day gradient drives **both** the emissive multiplier and the bloom intensity and threshold. At night, neon always sits at about 2–4× the threshold, and hover adds more.
- The specular contribution to the bloom input is clamped (or `luminanceSmoothing` raised), so golden-hour gold bevels don't sparkle.
- The neon tube shader has a **built-in fake glow**, so night still reads on the low tier without bloom. The glow is either a fresnel falloff on slightly oversized shells, or additive halo ribbons generated in Blender along the same curves.
- **Light spill** comes from:
  - the `_NIGHT` bake (D-031);
  - **additive light-pool decals** under lanterns and along paths;
  - a dim, cool **moon** directional light.
- The wisp's point light is **always in the scene** (intensity 0 when hidden), and the maximum dynamic light count is fixed at boot.

*Replaces:* the spec's rule that only hovered objects' emissive rises above the threshold, R-006's bloom policy, and R-007's "emissives carry night".

### D-039 Water (S-5)
**Decision:**
- On mid and high tiers, a **quarter-res mirrored pass of only neon and emissives** (the neon batch, lit windows and the sky) gives the refs' night reflection streaks. It's blurred vertically and distorted by the water normal.
- A **river depth / shore-distance texture** baked from the Blender terrain drives depth tint, shoreline foam and the teal-to-lavender gradient.
- The low tier keeps env reflection plus Fresnel only.

*Why:* Emissive streaks with no reflection source can't know where the neon is. D-017's draw-call worry also predates batching: a batched neon-only pass is a couple of draws.
*Supersedes:* D-017.

### D-040 Ambient light and the env map (S-6)
**Decision:**
- `scene.environment` supplies **both specular and diffuse** ambient, with no `HemisphereLight` on mid and high tiers. The low tier may swap to a hemisphere light with env diffuse zeroed by injection, but never both at once.
- The env cube renders at **256 px** from a small **env scene**, so gold and glossy cream have something to reflect. It contains:
  - the sky gradient;
  - an HDR warm sun-glow lobe;
  - horizon cloud bands;
  - a **dark-green ground hemisphere**;
  - optionally, a low-poly silhouette ring of town and forest.
- It still regenerates via `pmrem.fromCubemap(tex, reusedTarget)` when the sun moves about 1°. In r186 the PMREM is sized from the source cube, so 64 px would be too blurry; r187 adds a 256 px floor.

*Refines:* D-012, R-007.

### D-041 Shadow policy (S-2, C-1)
**Decision** (a default; spike 5 confirms it with measurements):
- **Mid and high tiers re-render the shadow map every frame.** With D-032's granularity that's about 10–15 sub-draws at 2048².
- If spike 5 shows that's too costly, fall back to static shadows while idle:
  - sway is excluded from the depth pass;
  - hover lift may detach from its shadow, which reads as levitation.
- Canopy dapple motion may be faked with a scrolling light-cookie term on receivers.
- The shadow frustum is fit to the **visible** (view-offset) frustum. Its center is texel-snapped and its size is quantized in power-of-two steps, so zooming doesn't shimmer.

*Replaces:* R-007's "updated only on pan or sun change".

### D-042 Antialiasing and sub-pixel detail (S-7)
**Decision:**
- **The mid LOD is responsible for removing sub-pixel detail:** fewer and larger Voronoi cells, closed-off lattices, and neon tubes thickened with distance. These are script params per tier.
- Spike 3 compares **DPR 1.5 + MSAA 4×** against **DPR 2 + SMAA** on the Air-class proxy, and whichever is steadier in motion wins.
- Cutout foliage uses `alphaToCoverage` when MSAA is on. The camera never jitters.

*Replaces:* the research note "no MSAA or SMAA at DPR 2".

### D-043 Renderer config and shader-compile hygiene (S-8, C-15, N-6)
**Decision:**
- **Canvas setup:** `<Canvas flat gl={{ antialias: false, alpha: false }}>`, with `gl.shadowMap.type = PCFShadowMap` set by hand and tone mapping owned by the composer (PBR Neutral). Otherwise R3F defaults to ACES, MSAA on the default framebuffer, and the removed `PCFSoftShadowMap`.
- **Shadows:** `renderer.shadowMap.enabled` is set once at boot and **never toggled**, because it's in every program's cache key. Tiers turn shadows off with `light.shadow.intensity = 0` and by skipping map updates.
- **Shader warm-up:**
  - `compileAsync` runs with a render target bound that matches the composer's input buffer (HalfFloat, linear).
  - One forced `shadow.needsUpdate` render happens during loading, behind the cream ocean, so the shadow-depth programs compile too.
- `BatchedMesh.setColorAt` is called before the first render or warm-up.
- `webglcontextlost` and `webglcontextrestored` are handled from day one.

### D-044 No `discard` in shared materials (S-9)
**Decision:**
- **The reveal clips nothing.** The town rises through the **opaque** cream ocean, which hides what's below it by depth. The glossy cream band is a **color and roughness blend** above `revealHeight`, and `revealHeight` stays in every material (D-018).
- **Mid-LOD foliage is opaque geometry clumps.** Any cutout cards go in their own late-drawn batch.
- **Draw order** is opaque, then alpha-tested, then translucent.

*Why:* Apple GPUs are TBDR. A `discard` anywhere in a shader makes its fragments ones that hidden-surface removal can't cull, which would tax the whole town every frame for a 2.5 s effect.
*Refines:* D-018, R-005.

### D-045 Foliage shading (S-14)
**Decision:** The material composer gets a foliage feature with:
- normals transferred from a proxy volume (a sphere or ellipsoid per clump, via Blender Data Transfer), so clumps shade as soft masses;
- a cheap translucency and back-light term (wrap lighting × view-to-sun alignment × a thickness proxy), which carries the golden-hour look;
- per-instance hue and value jitter;
- an `_AO` gradient toward the clump's core;
- low specular.

D-012's toon-ramp experiment sits behind the same feature flag.

### D-046 Color grading and height fog (S-15)
**Decision:**
- A small grade effect runs on all tiers, merged into the existing `EffectPass`. It covers lift/gamma/gain, saturation and a warm/cool split-tone. Its params are set **per keyframe** and interpolated by the gradient.
- Three's distance fog is replaced by **height + distance fog** injected via the composer. Its color is sampled from the sky gradient in the view direction, which also hides the forest horizon seam.

### D-047 Delivery, budgets and prerender (S-10, C-12)
**Decision:**
- **Budgets are stated per entry route and network profile,** for example "`/` on Fast 4G: sky within 1.5 s", with a matching line for `/cortico/platform`. Spike 6 sets the numbers.
- **The `.glb` compression workaround:** Vercel doesn't compress `model/gltf-binary`. Spike 6 picks a fix, such as separate `.bin` buffers served as an allowlisted type or a confirmed `Content-Type` override, and measures the bytes actually transferred.
- **Deep links:** the `/cortico` hero stream is on the critical path for `/cortico/*` deep links, so it must fit that route's budget.
- **Unloading:** R-011's "don't unload" holds for Milestone 1. Revisit it at 3+ neighborhoods: nine at hero density is about 20–35 MB, and iOS Safari loses WebGL contexts under memory pressure.
- **Prerender:**
  - three, R3F and the scene stay out of the SSR module graph, behind a client-only lazy boundary.
  - Links use `<Link prefetch="intent">`.
  - React Router v8 needs React ≥ 19.2.7, Vite ≥ 7 and Node ≥ 22.22, and is ESM-only.

*Refines:* D-028, R-011.

### D-048 Input sources (S-11, C-13)
**Decision:**
- The input layer normalizes three sources into one zoom delta that feeds the detent:
  - `wheel` + `ctrlKey` in Chromium and Firefox;
  - Safari's `gesturestart`/`gesturechange`/`gestureend`, with `preventDefault` on `gesturestart` so the page doesn't zoom;
  - pointer events on the iPad.
- Until mouse-wheel notches are handled, `+`/`−` keys and a visible in-world zoom affordance keep mouse users from getting stuck.

*Refines:* D-006. Its mapping stands; only the note that pinch arrives as `ctrl+wheel` was wrong.

### D-049 Milestone 1 sequencing: spikes, then a vertical slice, then breadth (S-12)
**Decision:** Milestone 1 runs in three phases:
1. **Seven timeboxed spikes**, 1–3 days each, with results logged here.
2. **A vertical slice:** the Cortico forum and one pedestal-to-panel flow, at golden hour and at night.
3. **Breadth:** the rest of Cortico, the town skeleton, forest, reveal, navigation, content, accessibility and tiers.

The checklist is in [spec.md](spec.md#milestone-1-one-exciting-neighborhood-cortico).
*Why:* The biggest unknown is whether scripted buildings can be as beautiful as the boards. It should be answered first, not last.

### D-050 Smaller rendering details (C-1 to C-4, C-8, C-9, C-11, N-4, N-5)
- **Panel view offset (C-1):**
  - Re-apply `setViewOffset` on every resize, and animate the offset with the panel.
  - Don't use `backdrop-filter` on the panel, because Safari re-blurs it every frame. Use a solid or pre-tinted sheet instead.
  - *Refines D-022.*
- **LOD swaps (C-2):** swap mid-LOD to high-LOD hero geometry with `setGeometryIdAt` mid-flight, while motion masks it, or with a short dithered crossfade. Reserve high-LOD vertex capacity up front.
- **Depth precision (C-3):**
  - Keep geometry within a bounded far plane, and let fog and a horizon skirt do the rest.
  - No `logarithmicDepthBuffer`, because it kills early-Z and HSR.
  - Use `reversedDepthBuffer` only if Safari supports `EXT_clip_control`.
- **Forest cards (C-4):** keep neighborhood vantages from framing the card band at a new pitch, or bake the cards at two pitches. *Refines R-010.*
- **Glass (C-8):** never use `transmission`. Fake the glass with env reflection, Fresnel alpha and a tinted interior card, in its own late, non-shadow-casting batch.
- **Glossy cream (C-9):** try `clearcoat` on hero-LOD cream only, budgeted as its own program.
- **Display P3 (C-11):** check whether the pmndrs composer supports it, and adopt it for the neon mint if it does.
- **Sorting (N-4):** leave `sortObjects` on for small counts, because it helps early-Z on non-Apple GPUs.
- **Real-time sun (N-5):** the browser timezone gives an IANA zone, not coordinates, so ship a table mapping each zone to a representative latitude and longitude.

### D-051 No low-end device testing for now
**Decision:** @bubbles isn't testing on real low-end devices for now. There's no used Air and no cloud device service. The `?perf=base` proxy on the M1 Max is the only performance check, with the review's conservative budget: ≤ 3 ms GPU at 2560×1600, which leaves room for the Max's ~4–6× advantage over a base Air. Spike 3 shrinks to Max-only work: the AA choice and the sub-draw budget.
*Why:* Keep momentum on the look. The budget already assumes the worst case.
*Later:* calibrate on real hardware before launch (Launch essentials), including a mid-range Android and in-app browsers.
*Refines:* D-035 (its calibration step is deferred).

## 2026-09-27: Milestone 1 spikes

### D-052 Spike 0 result: the scaffold's measurement and tooling
**Decision:**
- **Toolchain:** pnpm 12.6 pinned through `packageManager` and run via `corepack pnpm`; TypeScript 7, Vite 8, Vitest 5, Biome 2 (2-space, single quotes, no semicolons), Playwright on SwiftShader Chromium.
- **GPU ms** is one `TIME_ELAPSED` query around the whole R3F frame (R3F `addEffect` / `addAfterEffect`, stats-gl initialised on the canvas rather than patching `renderer.render`), so every pass, the shadow map included, is in the number.
- **Counters** accumulate across passes (`renderer.info.autoReset = false`, reset once per frame). **Sub-draws** are tallied by wrapping `renderBufferDirect` and summing each `BatchedMesh`'s `_multiDrawCount`, only where `WEBGL_multi_draw` exists; without it three already issues real calls.
- **Context restore:** a restore counter keys anything holding one-shot GL work (the env map render, the timer-query extension), because three's own rebuild doesn't redo it.
- Dev tooling (HUD, leva) shows in dev builds and with `?hud` / `?panel` in production.
*Why:* D-035 needs whole-frame GPU time and a sub-draw count that `renderer.info` can't give, and D-043 needs restore to actually recover.
*Refines:* D-035, D-043.

### D-053 Spike 1+2 result: the Blender → three round trip works end to end
**Decision:**
- **Pipeline contract (S-13):**
  - Blender 5.2.2 LTS runs headless with factory settings; `build.py` refuses any other version.
  - Procedures live in `assets/blender/folia/*.py` plus one `<hood>/<object>.py` per asset, and params in the asset's `.json`.
  - `palette.ts` reaches Blender as JSON from the Node build, so Python never parses TypeScript.
  - `assets/manifest.json` records a per-asset content hash (sources, the pack toolchain's versions). `pnpm assets:check` in the gate fails on a stale committed GLB, without needing Blender.
- **Baked light (B-1):**
  - Cycles vertex bakes into POINT attributes on the assembled asset, before it is split: `_AO` from the AO pass (2.5 m), and `_NIGHT` from a DIFFUSE direct + indirect pass with a black world and the neon as the only emitter.
  - Both are smoothed with three edge-neighbour passes, because one noisy sample per vertex streaks across long triangles.
  - Terrace top rings crowd toward the rim (`rim_bias`), where contact AO and spill change fastest.
  - On Metal the bake is ~3–4 s at 128 samples for ~100k vertices, mostly fixed cost. The first run compiles kernels (~2 min, then cached), and the CPU fallback costs ~0.2 s per sample.
- **Attribute storage (S-1, refines D-033):**
  - Each custom attribute has one fixed storage: `_ID` u8, `_AO` u8 normalized, `_NIGHT` u16 normalized over a range of 4.
  - Over-bright spill is scaled per vertex so it keeps its hue.
  - POSITION is quantized at 16 bits and NORMAL at 10, over a per-mesh volume.
  - The pack step doesn't use gltf-transform `meshopt()`: at `medium` it quantizes an `_` attribute only when its values happen to fit [-1, 1].
  - The validator runs on the raw export and again on the packed one.
- **Granularity (B-2):**
  - One geometry per (asset × material × LOD).
  - `_ID` groups (ground, terrace, canopy, shell, planting) index `uGroupState`.
  - The fragment draws as 5 batches: 23 calls including shadows and post, 9 sub-draws.
- **Runtime:**
  - GLTFLoader + MeshoptDecoder. Each primitive is dequantized to Float32 in world space and its custom attributes renamed (`groupId`, `bakedAo`, `bakedNight`).
  - Composer features: `baked`, `group`, `group-lift`, `foliage`. The program cache key is the feature keys. The depth material carries the lift.
  - Neon is MeshBasicMaterial scaled by the keyframe's emissive.
- **Forms:**
  - **Voronoi canopy:** a seeded jittered triangular lattice stands in for "remesh, triangulate". Then Dual Mesh, a per-cell inset proportional to √area, deleted centres, umbrella shaping, solidify and subdivide. The cells over the trunk stay closed as a crown.
  - **Shells:** lofted, cupped petals instead of metaballs or a voxel remesh. They're cleaner and seedable, with no remesh resolution to tune.
  - **Foliage:** normals come analytically from each clump's ellipsoid instead of a Data Transfer. Leaves are single-sided cards drawn DoubleSide, with the back face keeping the proxy normal.
- **Fast loop:**
  - A dev Vite plugin watches each asset's sources, rebuilds in a fresh child process, and hot-swaps the GLB inside a transition, with no reload.
  - Measured **7.6 s save → pixels**, against the 10 s target. A CLI rebuild is 4.8–5.4 s.
- **Size:** the packed fragment is 1.16 MB, about 132k triangles (cream 106k).
- **Look:** credible, not yet the boards.
  - The forms carry the vocabulary. The canopy's dappled shadow on the terraces already reads like the boards.
  - The gaps, largest first:
    1. No atmosphere at golden hour: a lit object in a dark void, with no horizon or height fog (D-046 isn't built).
    2. The cream leans salmon, with mauve shade.
    3. The greenery sits in balls instead of spilling.
    4. The canopy is too regular and has no branch ribs.
    5. Night is one flat navy with a single neon run.
    6. The shell's petals interpenetrate.
  - Most of the gap is light and material, not scripted form. Whether to stop and fix the look before spike 3 is @bubbles' call (D-049).
- **Deferred:**
  - Neon's built-in fake glow (D-038).
  - `revealHeight` in the composer (D-018).
  - Instance colours before first render (D-043), since no batch uses them yet.
  - The sun azimuth as keyframe data rather than a constant in `sun.ts`.

*Why:* B-1, B-2, S-1 and S-13 each needed a working asset to answer rather than a plan.
*Refines:* D-031, D-032, D-033, D-034, D-045.

### D-054 Foliage detail: lobes, sprays and a leaf-normal blend
**Decision:** Foliage clumps stop reading as one ball of big cards (@bubbles: "low-poly and lego-like"). Three scales, all in `assets/blender/folia/foliage.py` and driven by the `planting` params:
- **Lobes:** each clump is a few overlapping ellipsoids. The proxy normals come from their soft union (a softmin-weighted blend of each lobe's gradient), not from one ellipsoid.
- **Sprays:** leaves come in fans of about 7 around a point. They share a facing, so they shade together as a middle scale between lobe and leaf.
- **Leaves:** small, pointed and creased along the midrib, with a drooping tip. Each is 6 vertices and 4 triangles.
- **Normals:** each normal is 70% proxy and 30% the leaf's own creased normal, so the clump still shades as a mass while single leaves catch the light.
- **Colour:** the foliage feature's jitter is smooth value noise instead of an axis-aligned cell hash, plus clump-scale patches tinted toward `palette.lawn` as new growth.

**Cost:**
- The fragment's foliage goes from 11.2k to 36.0k triangles, and the GLB from 1.16 MB to 1.60 MB.
- **Not yet verified against the D-051 budget.** Perf is GPU ms under `?perf=base` (D-035), and that measurement is spike 3's. The dev HUD still pins at the 120 Hz vsync cap on the M1 Max, which shows no regression large enough to drop frames, and nothing more. stats-gl's GPU readout was too noisy to quote.
- Leaf-scale shimmer under camera motion is a risk for spike 3's AA choice (D-042, fol-hch).

**Placement:** sprays sit on a jittered Fibonacci lattice per lobe, not at random. At about 1× coverage, random placement leaves Poisson gaps, and those show as a hollow crown where a clump faces the camera.

*Why:* the art direction spends detail at the neighbourhood vantage. Foliage was 8% of the fragment's triangles, so it had room.
*Refines:* D-045.
